# Book/game continuity, side stories and returning characters

## How should the app distinguish books from games?

### Takeaway
Use distinct continuities and make the games' continuation status explicit. Sapkowski supplied a particularly useful direct written statement, avoiding reliance on secondhand summaries of his position.

### Cited Findings
- Sapkowski's complete written answer separates adaptations from his printed literary canon while praising the first game's quality. This is the principal authority for the continuity distinction. — [gram.pl, 10 February 2012](https://www.gram.pl/news/2012/02/10/tylko-u-nas-andrzej-sapkowski-nie-uznaje-gier-z-serii-wiedzmin-za-kanoniczna-kontynuacje-swoich-dziel.shtml)
- The end of The Lady of the Lake involves the Rivia pogrom, Geralt's injury, Yennefer's attempted healing and departure by boat. The app avoids declaring a definitive interpretation of the resulting otherworldly scene. — [Plot reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lady_of_the_Lake_(Sapkowski_novel))
- Netflix announced an adaptation of Sapkowski's books, not a sequel to CDPR's first game. — [Netflix's original announcement](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/the-witcher-saga-coming-to-netflix)

### Inferences
- Keep labels such as “book event”, “game event” and “player-dependent outcome”; this is an editorial organisation derived from the distinction above.

### Gaps
- No primary creative explanation was established for every difference in Triss/Yennefer screen time. The app states this explicitly instead of supplying production speculation.
- No primary translator statement establishing the popular “Buttercup sounded too feminine” explanation was obtained. The app deliberately omits it as a documented motive.
- Full primary novel text was not consulted. Detailed literary plot cross-checks use labelled community references; their evidentiary status is visible in the source drawer.

## Is there a missing story between W1 and W2?

### Takeaway
No essential book, mini-series or side game has to be inserted between them. The direct continuation is documented by CDPR itself.

### Cited Findings
- Marcin Blacha says W2 begins just after the first game and names Triss, Dandelion and Zoltan as returning acquaintances. — [CDPR, 3 April 2012](https://www.thewitcher.com/us/en/news/585/new-video-find-out-what-happened-in-the-first-witcher-game)
- Crossroads of Ravens instead follows Geralt at eighteen. — [Hachette announcement](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/orbit-books/acquisition-announcement-crossroads-of-ravens-by-andrzej-sapkowski/)
- The Price of Neutrality is framed as a ballad and takes place before the first campaign. It is a separate choice-driven adventure, with Deidre as Eskel's child of surprise. — [Adventure reference](https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Price_of_Neutrality)
- The Enhanced Edition's other official premium adventure is Side Effects, concerning Dandelion's trouble in Vizima. — [Enhanced Edition description](https://www.worthplaying.com/article/2018/4/23/news/108349-the-witcher-enhanced-edition-free-on-gogcom/)
- Thronebreaker follows Meve during the earlier war era; Rogue Mage's official premise is hundreds of years before Geralt. — [Thronebreaker chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thronebreaker:_The_Witcher_Tales); [Rogue Mage official page](https://www.playgwent.com/en/rogue-mage)
- The Adventure Game is a separate tabletop format with four playable heroes. — [CDPR announcement](https://www.cdprojekt.com/en/media/news/global-powerhouse-to-publish-the-witcher-adventure-game/)

### Inferences
- For this catch-up purpose, the ending and opening of the RPGs plus W2's memory revelations provide the useful bridge. Optional works can sit on a separate shelf.

### Gaps
- The app deliberately avoids an exact date for Side Effects: the accessible evidence did not establish one reliably.
- The Price of Neutrality's stated 1232 date creates known compatibility problems with book-character meetings. The app uses “before the first game” and does not manufacture a harmonised master calendar.

### Final scope check: comics and animation
- The official CDPR App Store listing confirms The Witcher 2 Interactive Comic Book was promoted as an introduction to the world before playing W2. That wording is not an in-world chronology claim. — [CDPR publisher listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/the-witcher-2-interactive-comic-book/id514528113)
- GOG's indexed official description identifies Reasons of State as a standalone interactive comic set during the First Nilfgaard War. The existing inter-game article now places it in earlier background; no exact year is imposed. Current requests can redirect to the latest game edition, where the older bonus description is absent. — [Official GOG bonus description](https://www.gog.com/en/game/the_witcher_3_wild_hunt?countryCode=CD&currencyCode=USD)
- The animated video announced in April 2012 is expressly a first-game recap. The article distinguishes a recap from a new intervening episode. — [Official CDPR announcement](https://www.thewitcher.com/us/en/news/585/new-video-find-out-what-happened-in-the-first-witcher-game)
- Matters of Conscience is listed among GOG's official bonus files, establishing its official distribution. Secondary descriptions and reproduced introductory text place its Vergen/Saskia story after W2. This limited final check did not retrieve a public primary publisher chronology statement or the official comic itself; the app therefore does not add a detailed timeline claim based only on those secondary descriptions. — [GOG bonus-file listing](https://www.gog.com/en/game/the_witcher_3_wild_hunt?countryCode=CD&currencyCode=USD); [CDPR comic portal, sign-in required](https://www.thewitcher.com/comicbook/en/)

## Editorial audit and additional primary evidence

### Cited findings
- Artur Ganszyniec, the first game's former lead story designer, directly explains the amnesia reset, the late protagonist change, the omission of Yennefer/Ciri and Alvin's thematic role in his mod-approved 2025 AMA. The main page returned the questions and full answers; individual comment-link fetches failed. These are retrospective developer recollections, not additional fictional scenes. — [AMA](https://www.reddit.com/r/witcher/comments/1njh4s4/i_was_the_lead_story_designer_for_the_witcher_1/), especially replies linked as `/comment/newwqb9/` and `/comment/newwjoa/`.

### Editorial changes
- Added `documented-adaptation-decisions` and source `c-ganszyniec-ama` to the comparison data. This updates the earlier gap: a primary explanation is now established for the first game's broad narrative focus, while no blanket motive is established for every later Triss/Yennefer decision.
- Emhyr's Duny/Ciri identity was removed from the default character dossier and retained in the opt-in `family-before-games` article with `c-emhyr` and `c-lady-lake` source IDs. His default dossier retains political background.
- The audit found no later W3 quest resolutions, deaths, war endings or romance outcomes in the main recap text. W2's own returning-memory revelations remain in the W2 recap. Books and game-specific additions remain distinguished.
- The AMA also offers expressly labelled personal headcanon concerning Alvin's genetics. That speculation is excluded from the factual account.

## Which people and choices should the reader remember?

### Takeaway
The data contains 25 character entries and, after the editorial addition, 12 continuity articles, ending before Wild Hunt's later events. Each entry has source IDs; community sources are honestly identified.

### Cited Findings
- Roche's and Iorveth's Flotsam crossroads are explicitly mutually exclusive. — [Official Prima guide](https://primagames.com/eguides/the-witcher-2-assassins-of-kings-eguide/walkthrough/chapter-1-flotsam)
- The official Adventure Game lore booklet provides concise background for Geralt, Triss, Dandelion, Dijkstra, Nilfgaard and the Lodge. — [Official booklet, University of Waterloo mirror](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~dtompkin/archive/dtlib/base/The%20Witcher%20Adventure%20Game.pdf)
- Letho's account connects the Hunt pursuit and Yennefer to the game-only history. The final confrontation can end in combat or release. — [Letho reference](https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Letho); [W2 plot cross-check](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witcher_2:_Assassins_of_Kings)
- Shani's guardianship and romance are conditional; the second game's journal accounts for their separation on that branch. — [Shani reference with journal text](https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Shani)
- Síle's sabotaged megascope allows a conditional rescue. — [Síle reference](https://witcher.fandom.com/wiki/Sheala_de_Tancarville)

### Inferences
- The app should never silently choose one save as the true outcome. Branch notes and conditional wording are required where an entry references survival, romance or political settlement.

### Gaps
- Several Fandom pages returned 402 on direct fetch; their indexed text was accessible through web search. The source drawer labels those pages as community references, not CDPR statements.
- The official W2 quest handbook is indexed at the following URL but direct web fetch failed: https://dlc-patcher.thewitcher.com/Bonus/Quest_Handbooks/The_Witcher_2_English_Quest_Handbook/The_Witcher_2_-_Guide_-_134x190.pdf . This continuity file does not claim to have read the entire PDF.
- Netflix story details, speculative adaptation motives, W3 character deaths and later quest resolutions were intentionally excluded from the character text.
