## The contract

The Witcher 3 opens with Geralt on Yennefer's trail, and it assumes you remember who she is, why Ciri matters and what happened at Loc Muinne. Two earlier games and eight books stand behind that scene, and most people who start the third game have played neither or forgotten both. The Wolf's Path is the catch-up I wanted: what happened in the first two games, who the returning faces are, and what the books add that the games never show.

## The journal

The recaps read like the quest journal they were modelled on. The Witcher is told in ten chapters and The Witcher 2 in eleven, each with its own piece of game art, a recap long enough to actually remind you, and a closing box of what to carry into the third game. Where the games split on a choice, the page says so and shows both sides. Roche and Iorveth get separate routes, and no playthrough is treated as the real one, including mine.

Twenty-six characters sit in a glossary of Witcher 3 journal portraits, each with the history that matters before the Wild Hunt and the single thing to remember about them.

## The books

The third game leans hard on Sapkowski's saga. Ciri's blood, the elves behind the Hunt, the emperor's secret, the family Geralt built before any game began. Eleven comparisons set the books against the first two games, side by side, and end with what Witcher 3 expects you to already know.

Those pages spoil the whole saga, so they stay locked until you choose to open them. That lock holds everywhere: on the cards, in search results, and on a link someone sends you directly. Being sent a chapter should never mean being told how the books end.

## The look

It is dressed as the game's own menus rather than a reading app. Near-black worn surfaces, thin frames with gold corners, parchment text in Alegreya, gold for anything you can click and red only for markers. The art runs in its real colours at full strength, from the Remastered key art on the front page to the GWENT paintings behind the book pages. The icons are the game's too: the School of the Wolf medallion, the five Signs, the yellow main quest marker.

Progress, bookmarks and the spoiler switch live in the browser, so it remembers where you stopped without an account. React, plain JavaScript, Vite and CSS. No backend.
